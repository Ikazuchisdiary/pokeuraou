=== READING OF THE THREE NULLS ===
The three results are not three failures; they are one measurement of where the pipeline's
signal dies, and the record contains the control that localises it.  FIRST, THE LOCALISATION.
The cleanest natural experiment in the whole record is leaf ranking, because it was applied in
two different places. At MATCH time it is +2.9 [+0.5, +5.3] over 1,675 games — significant.
Routed through TEACHER DATA, as a genuinely paired 12,000-game regeneration, it is +0.6 over
1,696 games — not significant. Same intervention, same direction, same instrument, one link
apart in the pipeline. The hidden bench has the same shape: at play time the information is
worth 0.960 points per opening decision and changes the advice in 36 of 40 openings; routed
through teacher data it is +0.9, then +0.8 after three leaks closed. So the pattern is not
"correctness does not matter". It is that correctness applied at PLAY time changes a decision
directly, while correctness applied to teacher data must survive a training step — and the
training step is where it dies. The record already measured why: training-seed spread is 0.0240
held-out AUC against 0.004 between settings, six times larger than the difference being judged,
and each configuration was trained once. T1 is the worked example — the only pre-floor
"significant" data lever (-3.5) became -1.2 once the floor was built. A better teacher position
produces a model whose difference from its predecessor is smaller than the noise of retraining
it. That is the binding constraint on the board pipeline, and it is upstream of the board, not
on it.  SECOND, AND MORE IMPORTANT: the board is measuring the wrong axis, and the record
supports saying so. Four things converge. (1) The instrument resolves +/-2.4 at 1,696 games and
every lever judged on the floor since it was built lands inside that band except one. So most of
the "no effect" list is a list of bounds, not of zeros — which means those levers cannot be
ORDERED, not that they are absent. (2) The largest board number in the record is not a strength
property. The book is +13.6/+18.9 against a uniform drawer and +2.1 against a drawer who also
has it, intervals overlapping. Its measured value is exploitation of a straw opponent, and T7
records that the rating scale cannot hold it at all. The biggest number the project has is a
measure of how bad its baseline opponent is. (3) I confirmed from data/train-gen10-s0.log that
the book — that same largest lever — is built 100% from turn-1 cells, and turn 1 is the value
function's worst turn by a wide margin: AUC 0.681 (n=11,644) against 0.958 by turn 10. And two
independent measurements this session say the resulting advice is close to a coin flip between
training seeds of the same configuration: per-cell sd 6.3-6.6 points against a within-row spread
of 0.09, mixture total variation 0.77-0.91, lead mass moving 66-86 points. The product's
headline output is not reproducible, and no board match can see that, because both seeds play
equally well. (4) The shipped values are miscalibrated against the population that matters by
far more than the board can resolve: claimed 42.3% vs measured 34.0% against BIG6, claimed 54.3%
vs measured 32.5% against Ben Madigan, a gap E2 halved (-10.3 -> -4.7) and did not close.
Notably the by-turn table shows the model is near-calibrated ON ITS OWN self-play distribution
at turn 1 (model mean 0.524 against an actual 53.2%), so this is off-distribution error — the
value function has never seen tournament teams played well. The error in the shipped number is
three to nine times the largest effect the board instrument can resolve.  SO: yes, board win
rate is the wrong thing to be optimising, and the project's own first paragraph already says so
— the deliverable is equilibrium mixed strategies, adoption frequencies, EV loss and an
opponent-spread posterior, and strength is a means. The three nulls are the fourth instance of a
pattern the record names as its own brake, and the correct response is not to find a better data
lever or to buy more games. It is to stop spending 31-50 machine-minutes per unreadable answer
on training changes, and to redirect onto the two questions that are in the product's own units,
are currently failing, and are measurable off the board: whether the printed advice is
reproducible (cheap, half-measured already, failing badly), and whether the printed values are
true against real opponents (expensive, priced by the record itself at 2,000 games per side,
never paid). Board matches keep one legitimate use — play-time search configuration, where the
record's own evidence (width, match-time ranking, the learned leaf) says the board can actually
see the effect.  One discipline note, because it cuts against my own conclusion: "the board
cannot see it" is not "it is zero", and none of the three nulls licenses abandoning the teacher-
side work. The narrowing fix in particular took the equilibrium mass kept from 58.1% to 93.3%
and the sacrifice from 2.53 points to 0.05 — that is a real improvement to the object the
project ships, and it should be recorded as such in its own units, with the conversion to Elo
left unclaimed, since TODO.md states plainly that nobody owns that conversion.
